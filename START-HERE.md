# Start Here — Diamond King Learning Journey

**Learn → Imagine → Build → Share**

Welcome. This is a free, self-directed starter path for turning one question into a small, evidence-backed project. You do not need a paid course, subscription, or special software. A text editor and a place to keep your work are enough.

**Time suggestion:** one focused session of about 60–90 minutes, or four shorter sessions.

## Before you begin

Create a folder or repository for your work. Keep private information, passwords, API keys, personal records, and confidential conversations out of public files. Use fictional or non-sensitive examples when practising.

Create a note with these fields:

- Project name
- Date started
- What I want to learn
- Why it matters
- What I already know
- What I still need to verify

## Step 1 — Learn: define a useful outcome

Choose one skill or question. Make the outcome observable.

Weak outcome: “Learn AI.”

Better outcome: “Compare two ways to summarize a public article and record which summary preserves the source's main claims more accurately.”

**Exercise**
1. Write one question you want to answer.
2. List three things you need to understand.
3. Identify what evidence would show that you learned them.
4. Find a reliable source yourself and record its title, publisher, URL, and access date.
5. Separate facts from your assumptions.

**Completion evidence:** a short note with the question, learning outcome, source details, and three takeaways.

## Step 2 — Imagine: turn learning into an idea

Capture an idea without treating it as a proven solution.

Use this template:

| Biggest risk | |

**Exercise**
- Write one idea in your own words.
- Mark every uncertain statement as an assumption.
- Write one question that could prove the idea wrong.
- Choose one small experiment that does not expose private data or create financial risk.

**Completion evidence:** one idea record with at least one testable assumption and one proposed experiment.

## Step 3 — Build: make the smallest safe version

Do not start with a large platform. Build the smallest artefact that can answer your experiment question. It might be a sketch, checklist, one-page HTML file, spreadsheet, or written workflow.

**Exercise**
1. Write the single problem the artefact should solve.
2. List what is in scope and out of scope.
3. Make the simplest version.
4. Test it with fictional or public information.
5. Record what worked, what failed, and what you changed.

**Build checklist**
- [ ] The purpose is clear.
- [ ] The first version has one main task.
- [ ] Inputs and outputs are understandable.
- [ ] No secrets or sensitive personal data are included.
- [ ] A failure or limitation is visible to the user.
- [ ] The result can be reviewed by another person.

**Completion evidence:** a working small artefact or clearly labelled prototype, plus a short test record.

## Step 4 — Share: publish evidence, not hype

Prepare a short project note:

- What I tried
- What I built
- What I tested
- What the evidence shows
- What did not work
- What remains untested
- What I will do next
- Sources and licences

Share only information that is safe and appropriate for a public audience. Attribute third-party material according to its licence. Do not publish private chats, credentials, personal contact details, or another person's private information.

**Completion evidence:** a reviewable project note with links to the artefact and its sources.
